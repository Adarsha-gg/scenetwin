## The outcome (not a p-value): SceneTwin senses *fabrication*, and its two signals fail on different inputs

The product question for an AD audit metric is not "what's the correlation" — it's
**"would it notice if the description lied?"** A hallucinated AD (fluent, confident,
right length, wrong facts) is the worst failure for a blind viewer: there is no way to
catch it from the soundtrack. We stress-tested SceneTwin against exactly this, with a
control that the earlier retracted "dual-signal dominance" claim lacked.

## Setup (60 external clips, same-length rewrites)

For each clip we took the human **expert AD** and produced two same-length twins via the
grader:
- **hallucination** — change 2-3 concrete visual facts to plausible falsehoods
  (e.g. *bald → long-haired*, *recording studio → concert hall*, *cymbal → snare drum*).
- **faithful paraphrase** (control) — reword, change nothing factual.

Mean length delta ±0.8 words, so detection cannot be a verbosity artifact. Each twin is
scored against the frames by **ADQA** (frame-grounded QA, Gemini) and **CLIP** (visual
grounding, local). A detector should flag the hallucination and ignore the paraphrase.

## Result 1 — ADQA has a structural hallucination blind spot

| | catches hallucination | ties (blind) | false-alarm on paraphrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADQA-only** | 70% | **28% (17/60)** | 18% |

ADQA is well-calibrated (low false-alarm) but **blind on 17/60 clips**: when the
fabricated fact isn't one of the probed frame questions, the lie and the truth get the
*same* QA score. A QA-based AD metric cannot see a fabrication it never asked about.

## Result 2 — CLIP visual grounding is *fabrication-specific* and covers the blind spot

The honest part: CLIP's raw **sign** ("expert beats candidate") is biased — it also
ranks the expert above a faithful paraphrase 65% of the time. So you cannot use the raw
sign as a standalone gate. The usable signal is the **magnitude** of the grounding drop:

| grounding drop vs expert | fabrication | faithful paraphrase | paired test |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 60 clips | **+0.037** | +0.008 | p < 1e-4 |
| ADQA-blind clips (n=17) | **+0.035** | +0.006 | p = 1e-4 |
| CLIP catch-rate on ADQA-blind clips | **100%** | — | — |

Fabrication costs **~5× more visual grounding than a faithful paraphrase**, and the gap
holds *exactly on the 17 clips where ADQA goes blind* (CLIP catches 100% of them). So the
two signals are **complementary by construction, not redundant**: ADQA misses
out-of-question fabrications; CLIP's grounding drop catches them.

## Why this matters

- This is the **legitimate version of the dual-signal claim** that was retracted on
  2026-06-07. The retraction died on a *tie artifact* with no control. This result has
  the control (faithful paraphrase), uses **magnitude not sign**, and is significant on
  the subset that actually matters (ADQA's blind spots).
- It is direct **construct validity**: SceneTwin responds to *visual faithfulness*, not
  to wording or length. A 2-3 fact swap with identical length still moves the score.
- It is the architectural justification for the whole framework: you need *both* signals
  because they fail on different inputs.

## Honest caveats

- Not a turnkey deployment gate. The raw "expert beats candidate" sign false-alarms on
  benign paraphrases (CLIP 65%, ADQA 18%); a fielded detector would threshold the
  grounding-drop *magnitude*, calibrated against the paraphrase-level drop (~+0.008).
- Hallucinations are LLM-generated by the same family (Gemini) that grades — a stronger,
  independent grader (Opus/GPT) re-run is logged in [[findings/NEXT-STEPS]].
- n=60 OOD clips; CLIP catch CI95 [0.88, 1.0].
