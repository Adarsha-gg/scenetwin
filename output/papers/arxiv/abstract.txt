Audio description (AD) makes video accessible to blind and low-vision viewers by narrating visual information the soundtrack does not convey. As systems generate AD at scale, the bottleneck shifts from authoring to auditing: is a candidate AD visually grounded, does it omit or fabricate scene evidence, and which clips need scarce human review? Reference-based metrics are strong when a trusted professional AD exists, but newly generated AD for undescribed video usually lacks one. We present SceneTwin, a human-reference-free AD audit framework with three layers: a CLIP + frame-grounded ADQA score for ranking candidates against the video; a reference-free error-detection layer; and a TRIBE-v2 neural side-car for review triage. On a corrected three-tier ladder (cross-decoy < crowd caption < professional AD), SceneTwin reaches Spearman rho = 0.952 on a 60-clip primary set and 0.957 on an 18-clip pilot; a reference-style LLM-AD-Eval proxy nearly ties this but needs the professional reference SceneTwin avoids. A fused CLIP+ADQA detector flags fabricated visual facts (AUC = 0.904), and a lexical omission detector separates professional from corrupted descriptions (clip-level AUC = 0.945, pilot), revealing that even professional ADs state a probed key visual fact only 61.8% of the time. A CLIP grounding-drop gate detects hallucination (AUC = 0.835, 70% recall at 10% false-positive rate) and a raw-CLIP gate catches 98.3% of wrong-clip descriptions. The neural triage gap predicts audit failures (AUC = 0.794) and resists cheap text and visual-motion baselines. Combining or reweighting signals yields no gain, so the architecture stays deliberately modular. SceneTwin is an audit layer for hybrid AI+human AD workflows, not a replacement for professional describers or blind-user validation.
